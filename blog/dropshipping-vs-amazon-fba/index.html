Dropshipping vs Amazon FBA: Which Wins in 2026?

Published June 2026 · 9 min read · Affiliate links disclosed

Dropshipping and Amazon FBA are the two most-discussed entry points into ecommerce in 2026. Both are legitimate business models with real success stories — and both have failure modes that beginners consistently underestimate.

FactorDropshippingAmazon FBA
Startup CostUnder $100/month$1,000–$3,000+ inventory
Inventory RiskNoneHigh (storage fees if slow)
Typical Margin15–30%25–40%+ at scale
Customer OwnershipYou own the relationshipAmazon owns the customer
Shipping Speed2–7 days (US/EU suppliers)1–2 days (Prime)
Brand BuildingFull controlLimited
Acquisition AppealHigh (email list, own traffic)Moderate (Amazon-dependent)

Startup Cost

Dropshipping wins decisively. A Shopify plan plus Spocket or Zendrop runs under $100/month with zero inventory required. FBA requires sourcing inventory ($1,000–$3,000 minimum) before you've made a single sale.

Margins

FBA wins on potential — if you source well and scale volume, FBA margins can reach 25–40%+. Dropshipping margins typically run 15–30% after supplier cost. Before committing to either model, read our pricing strategy guide — the margin calculation is different for each.

Verdict for 2026

Start with dropshipping if you have limited capital and want to validate business concepts quickly. Consider FBA for a specific hero SKU once you've validated demand via dropshipping and have capital to invest. Ready to start? See how to set up your first store →
